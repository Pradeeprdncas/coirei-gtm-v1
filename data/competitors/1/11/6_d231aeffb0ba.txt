3 easy steps to a better period
1. Pinch it.
Wash your hands and pinch the disc in half. Once it's pinched, it's about the width of a tampon.
2. Insert and tuck.
Angle it down and back toward your tailbone, push it as far back as it goes, then tuck the front rim up behind your pubic bone. Then forget about it for up to 12 hours.
3. Hook and remove.
Hook a finger under the rim, pull slowly and keep it level to prevent any spillage. Pro tip: For a low-stress first removal, try taking your disc out while in the shower.

Honest answers to your most common questions
What if it doesn't fit my body?
Flex Disc currently comes in one size and was designed to fit a wide range of bodies. It sits in the vaginal fornix (just beneath your cervix) instead of in the canal, so it doesn't press on your vaginal walls the way a cup or tampon can. And the fornix just so happens to be the widest part of the vaginal canal. So if you’ve had trouble with a cup before, don’t be afraid of a disc based on how it looks. That said, Flex Disc does run on the larger side in terms of discs. If it's not right for your body, don’t give up on discs altogether. There may be another option in your drugstore aisle that works better.
Will it leak?
Flex Disc shouldn’t leak as long as it’s inserted correctly, fully surrounding your cervix. When you’re learning, we recommend wearing a liner for the first couple of days. Most leaks come from a disc that isn't pushed all the way back, isn’t fully tucked behind your pubic bone, or from overflow on a heavy day. If it empties a little when you use the bathroom, that's normal: your pelvic floor relaxes and the disc can slip forward, letting a little fluid out into the toilet. Just tuck it back up with a clean finger when you’re done.
What if I can't get it out?
Removal can take some practice, but don’t worry: your disc can’t get lost! If it feels hard to reach, try removing in the shower when your body is more relaxed, changing positions (like a low squat or a leg up on the toilet), or using your pelvic floor muscles to gently bear down and push the disc forward. Then just hook a finger under the rim and pull. There's no suction seal to break. If you're stuck, our Flexperts can walk you through it.
Can I use it with an IUD?
Lots of Flex Disc users have IUDs, and the disc doesn't use suction to stay in place, which may reduce the risk of accidental IUD expulsion. Every IUD sits a little differently, though, so check with your doctor or Ob/Gyn before using any internally worn product. They'll know how your specific IUD is positioned.
Is it really mess-free during sex?
Yes, but make sure to use a fresh disc before intercourse. Some people will see a little residual blood from your vaginal walls. As long as the disc is inserted correctly, it will be up and out of the way for sex, sitting beneath your cervix to catch any blood while leaving your vaginal canal clear. If your disc feels low at first or it causes any discomfort for you or your partner, you may just need more foreplay: your vaginal canal expands when you're aroused, and your disc will typically move farther back as that happens. If you continue to experience any discomfort, it’s best to stop, wait a bit, and remove your disc. If you use lube, make sure it’s water-based. After sex, wait 30 minutes to an hour before taking it out so your body can settle. Flex is not a contraceptive and doesn't protect against STIs. If you use condoms, friction with the disc can make them less effective, so use a backup method.
